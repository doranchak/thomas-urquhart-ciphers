Logopandecteision: 1653 vs 1834

Collation of Sir Thomas Urquhart's Logopandecteision (London, 1653) with the text reprinted in The Works of Sir Thomas Urquhart of Cromarty (Edinburgh: Maitland Club, 1834), focusing on the Proquiritations and the Cyphral Distich. Prepared 26 September 2026.

Are the Proquiritations unique to the 1834 reprint?
No — but the 1834 version is a different, revised text.

All 32 Proquiritations, with the same 32 signature initials and the same introductory "Algebraical Hexastick", are printed in the 1653 edition. In the 1834 reprint they appear in a different order (only №18, 24, 25, 31, 32 keep their position) and are substantially reworded — on average only about 74% of the word sequence is shared (range 30–97%).

Is the Cyphral Distich only in the reprint?
In every source examined here, yes: it first appears in the 1834 reprint (p. 417).

The 1653 copy examined ends Proquiritation 32 with a row of ornaments, the Parva peto couplet, FINIS, and the errata. There is no Distich, no "Of carping Zoil…" verse, and no first Postscript paragraph. Only one 1653 copy was examined (the British Library copy filmed for Early English Books); other surviving copies have not been checked.

Do we know that Urquhart wrote the revised Proquiritations and the Distich?
No — it is not established either way.

The Distich decodes only against the revised 1834 Proquiritations, so whoever composed it also wrote, or at least had in hand, that revised text. The 1834 editors say they "reprinted from the original editions" and mention no variant. The internal evidence (below) fits 1652–53 well, but it cannot rule out a later hand. The question can only be settled by finding a 1653 copy that contains the revised leaves.

Does the cipher work on the 1653 text?

No. Applying the same rule (number n in position i → first letter of word n of Proquiritation i) to the 1653 text gives nonsense:

1653: NTMTMNDN·OHTISEABRPTTSGSNTAATVTD
      OSOTHMOSTTTBHPBTHRIPFSWFLVWTABV·
1834: OGODUPHOLDKINGCHARLSTHESECONDAND
      MAKEHIMTHESUPREMERULEROFTHISLAND

(· = the number is larger than the paragraph's word count.) The Distich was therefore built against the revised text: its order, and its word counts. The revision and the cipher belong together.

What the revision changed

  • Fewer appeals to "the State". In 1653 most items ask "the State", "the Conqueror", "the noblest State in the world" or "the State of England" to act. The 1834 text mostly substitutes neutral phrases: "the present authority", "the power now in being", "any supreme authority of that nation", "those in whose power it is".
  • Explicit political details removed. Gone are "the most of Scotland is subdued… by the Conqueror" (1653 №5), "would at Sterling have debarred him… his going to Worcester" (1653 №20), and "the English now inhabiting Scotland" (1653 №27).
  • New period details added. 1834 №11 asks that "his person no longer [be] detained as a prisoner of war". Urquhart was captured at Worcester in 1651. 1834 №3 names "the covenanters, who are at this time accounted the State's adversaries". 1834 №17 speaks of long acquaintance with "many choice English gentlemen".
  • Stylistic expansion. Most 1834 items are longer, add doublets ("be not quenched and quite extinguished"), and vary the closing formula, which №32 then sums up.

Who wrote the revised Proquiritations and the Distich?

No document examined here settles this. The evidence:

  • The revised text is full of details that were live in 1652–53 and obscure by 1834: Urquhart held as a prisoner of war after Worcester, the covenanters "at this time accounted the State's adversaries", an English garrison in his house, the "subsequestrator" of his estates.
  • The Postscript that comes with it in 1834 carries a precise double-style dateline ("London, February 5, 1652 … 15, 1653"). It also describes the printing of the book (two printers, a friend acting as Corrector of the press), which matches the 1653 errata preface.
  • The hidden message, a prayer for Charles II as "supreme ruler", has an obvious 1653 motive: to conceal a royalist loyalty inside a petition to the Commonwealth. Encrypting it would have served little purpose in 1834.
  • The rewrite is integrated rather than cosmetic. The closing formulas were varied so that №32 could list all seven, and the text is consistent with the Hexastick's arithmetic of 32. That is a lot of careful work for a club editor to invent without comment.
  • Hurried printing by two printers, and at least one unusual copy (Craigston, with a portrait unknown elsewhere), make variant states of the final sheets plausible. Final leaves reset or replaced during printing (cancels) were common in 1650s books.
  • No 1653 copy containing the revised Proquiritations or the Distich has been identified. The copy examined here lacks both, as do others' checks of the British Library copy and the EEBO-TCP transcription.
  • The 1834 editors describe their text only as "reprinted from the original editions". They note one editorial change (the errata) but say nothing about a second state of the Proquiritations.
  • Even within the 1834 text, not everything is claimed as Urquhart's: the added Postscript says the "interstitiary verses" were written by a friend. The "Of carping Zoil" verse beneath the Distich could equally come from another hand.
What would settle it. Examine the final gathering of other surviving 1653 copies: those listed in ESTC / Wing U138, and especially Scottish-held copies such as National Library of Scotland, Edinburgh and Glasgow Universities, and the Craigston library copy the 1834 editors used for the portrait. Look for a Proquiritations section beginning "Seeing, from the creation of the world…" (K. F.), followed by the Distich and the dated Postscript. One such copy would show that both come from Urquhart's own time. It would also make his authorship far more likely, given the Postscript's own warning about the friend's verses. The Maitland Club's records of which copy it reprinted would also help.
