Method and findings

The rule. Number the 285 figures in order, counting straight through the eight lines and the closing ten-figure "Decagram". The k-th figure n points to page k of the 1652 Jewel. Count to its n-th word and take the first letter. The verse printed beneath the cipher points to this key: the "Decagram" is "all gather'd out of my Exskybalorum". The method was first published by Vals AI; this page reproduces it independently from the 1652 text.

  • One page is used twice. The cipher has 285 figures but the book has only 284 pages, so one page must supply two letters. The data show it is page 157: figures 157 and 158 both point there, and from figure 158 on every figure k points to page k−1. The last figure lands on the book's last page, 284.
  • Pages are counted in physical order, not by the printed page numbers. The 1652 printing misnumbers ten pages; for example, the 36th page is printed "34", and the 110th is printed "101".
  • Counting words. A word broken across two lines counts once. A hyphenated compound ("Marshal-general") counts as two words. "&" counts as a word. Punctuation does not count.
  • Results:
  • Slips. Where the count is off, a word starting with the needed letter almost always sits one or two places away. Ten of these slips happen on pages that open with the second half of a word carried over from the previous page. In print that fragment is the first thing on the page, and in those cases Urquhart counted it. Eleven more involve a hyphenated compound he counted as one word. The rest look like ordinary hand-counting slips. The blue outline on the page marks the intended word; the yellow highlight is the word the printed figure literally points to.
  • Line 4 enciphers "IRSH", not "IRISH". This may be a slip, or a deliberate contraction that keeps the line to ten syllables.
  • Line 5 (figures 149–157) does not decode. The printed figures spell C-O-N-E-R-T-H-T-O, and no word fits even allowing for counting slips or simple misprints of the figures. The reading shown, OVERTHROW, is a conjecture. It fits the sense and the nine-letter space, and it scans as two syllables ("o'erthrow"). The decoded letters also contain "O·ERTH·O" offset by one place, which suggests a slip in enciphering or in printing the figures. Checking an original 1652 copy that contains the cipher leaf would settle it.

Sources. Cipher figures transcribed from the reprint of the cipher in R. D. S. Jack and R. J. Lyall's 1983 edition of The Jewel, pp. 212–213 (photographs in this folder). The page and line text is the EEBO-TCP transcription A95749 (Thomason copy E1506_1, Wing U134), which preserves the 1652 page and line breaks. This is the same text served by the University of Michigan and the Oxford Text Archive. The filmed Thomason copy lacks the cipher leaf, and the 1834 Maitland Club reprint omits the cipher and uses different pagination. The 1983 edition's own page numbers cannot be the key, because the cipher needs 284 pages of the original.
